About FedCatalog

Federal software is scattered across FedRAMP, the DoD Cyber Exchange, GSA programs, cloud marketplaces, USAspending, contract vehicles and vendor websites. FedCatalog brings those public signals together so government teams, software vendors and systems integrators can answer a simpler question: what can government actually use, and how can it be bought?

What FedCatalog does

FedCatalog combines publicly available information covering FedRAMP offerings and their authorization records; FedRAMP agency authorization and reuse records; DoD cloud impact-level listings; GSA OneGov agreements; the cloud platforms each offering runs on; observed federal contract activity from USAspending; and links to marketplaces and acquisition sources.

What FedCatalog does not do

FedCatalog doesn’t rank vendors, sell placement, or decide what software is “best.” It organizes public information so buyers and sellers can understand the path from authorization to procurement.

FedCatalog does not certify software, issue security authorizations, award contracts, or determine whether a product is suitable for a particular government requirement.

Authorization, purchasing and marketplace availability are different things. FedCatalog keeps those distinctions explicit on every page, and the methodology explains what each figure means and where it came from.

Why I built it

I spent most of my career working in and around government technology, first as a Marine and later on the commercial side. I kept seeing the same problem: useful information exists, but it’s scattered across a dozen systems that were never designed to work together. FedCatalog is my attempt to make that information easier to find and understand.
